Harness Engineering · Module 17 · Capstone

Build → observe → evaluate → evolve

The whole course as one loop. Build a harness (Part I), make it observable (M12), evaluate it against a baseline (M08/M13), then evolve it from traces (M14) — and delete what the ablation proves dead (M09). Budget a day.

Section 1The four-phase loop

The capstone is the course as a loop. Each phase feeds the next; the dashed arrow is the deletion step closing it.

Section 2Phase checklist

PhaseDoWalkthrough
BUILDLoop + tools (count them) + context policy (offload) + handoff + verification + stop conditions + permission gatew01–w08

Section 3The deliverable

One page, dated, naming the model version:

  • What you built (HarnessCard fields filled).
  • What the trace showed (where tokens/cost went; one failure attributed).
  • Eval vs baseline (score, variance, delta).
  • What you deleted (and what surprised you).

That document is what you re-run against the next model release — and why deletion (Module 09) is the closing step, not optional cleanup.

Section 4Check your understanding

Quiz · Module 17

In the capstone loop, what closes the circle back to BUILD?
Why: EVOLVE = ablate + delete. The deletion step is what returns you to BUILD with a leaner harness — the ratchet turning both ways (Module 09).
Why run EVALUATE with n > 1 against a real model?
Why: Same as Module 08 — variance can swamp a single run's signal. Report variance or you haven't measured.
OBSERVE feeds EVOLVE because…
Why: AHE (Module 14) turns traces into evolution signal. No observability, no evolution loop — the phases are chained, not independent.
The one-page deliverable should name the model version because…
Why: Components encode assumptions about model weakness that expire on a schedule you don't control. Dating it makes the next re-run a diff, not a rebuild.
Your move
  1. Pick a real, small, verifiable task. Run BUILD→OBSERVE→EVALUATE→EVOLVE this week.
  2. Keep the one-pager. Open it the next time a model upgrades. That is the whole discipline.
