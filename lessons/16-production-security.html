Section 2Where humans stay in the loop

Not everything needs a human. But three classes of action should gate on a person:

  • Irreversible — deletes, force-pushes, dropped tables. No model decision is final here.
  • Externally visible — sends email, posts, commits to main, charges a card. The blast radius leaves your system.
  • High-cost — spins up expensive compute, calls a paid API at scale.

Microsoft's Agent Framework models this directly: "Don't ask again" standing approval rules plus heuristic auto-approval for safe, unattended execution. The design pattern: default to ask, earn don't-ask per tool through observed safety — the opposite of default-allow.

Section 3Rollback from the trace

Module 12's append-only log is also your rollback primitive. Because every step is recorded, a bad change can be reverted to the last known-good state by replaying the log minus the offending step. This is why observability and safety are the same subsystem viewed from different ends: the log that lets you attribute a failure also lets you undo it.

Irreversible actions route through a human, not the model.

Section 4Governance on the card

The HarnessCard's "Governance" field (Module 08) is where this lives: sandboxing, approvals, provenance logging. If you cannot fill it, your harness is not production-ready by the field's own standard. The research consensus (Zhong & Zhu's intervention recording, Anthropic's audit log, Microsoft's OTEL) is that governance is not a feature you add later — it is part of the runtime from day one, because retrofitting an append-only, attributable log onto a harness that wasn't built for it is painful.
