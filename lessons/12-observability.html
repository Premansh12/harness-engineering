Observability & tracing

A harness you cannot see is a harness you cannot improve. Observability — traces, token counts, replays, failure attribution — is the pillar that turns "it worked" or "it didn't" into "here is exactly why." This module covers what to instrument and why.

Section 1Why observability is a core pillar

The research papers are unanimous that observability is not optional. Zhong & Zhu list it as a component responsibility (Module 11). Lin et al. (Agentic Harness Engineering, Module 15) make it the entire method — three observability pillars drive their self-evolving loop. Microsoft's Agent Framework ships OpenTelemetry by default. The through-line: you cannot engineer what you cannot see.

Concretely, a production harness should emit, per run:

SignalWhat it tells you
Trace (every step)What the agent did, in order, with tool I/O
Token usageWhere context is going (Module 03)
CostPer run, per task — the other axis of Module 08
Latency per stepWhich tool or call is slow
Failure attributionWhich step caused the bad outcome
ReplayRe-run a step in isolation to debug

Section 2OpenTelemetry as the substrate

The field is converging on OpenTelemetry's generative-AI semantic conventions. The reason: a standard trace format means your harness, your eval framework, and your dashboard all speak the same language. Microsoft's Agent Framework adopts exactly this. The practical upshot — instrument once with OTEL spans, consume anywhere (Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, your own Grafana).

A trace is a tree you can attribute failures to a step and replay it.

Section 3Failure attribution

Tracing tells you what happened; attribution tells you why it broke. The maturity-ladder paper (Module 11) treats failure attribution as the difference between H1 and H2: without it, a failed run gives you a broken patch and no signal; with it, you get a cause. This is also the input to self-evolution (Module 15) — you cannot evolve a harness you cannot attribute failures in.

Section 4Replay and session history

Anthropic's managed-agents work makes the session log append-only and the compaction reversible: because every step is logged, you can rebuild the full context from the log even after a compaction dropped it. That is observability paying double — it debugs today and un-breaks yesterday. Keep an append-only log; treat compaction as a cache, not a destroyer.
