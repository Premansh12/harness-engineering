Harness Engineering · Module 14 · Research

Self-evolving harnesses

The frontier: harnesses that improve themselves from their own run traces. Two 2026 papers — Agentic Harness Engineering and HarnessX — show evolvable harnesses beating hand-designed ones. This is the stability/adaptability tension (Module 10) resolved in favour of adaptability, with observability as the engine.

Section 1The problem with manual harness engineering

Lin et al. (Agentic Harness Engineering, arXiv:2604.25850) open with why automation is hard: a heterogeneous action space across editable components, voluminous trajectories that bury signal, and edits whose effect is hard to attribute. You tweak a tool, run the agent, get a different number — but which part of the change caused it? Manual iteration drowns in exactly the noise Module 08 warned about.

Section 2AHE: three observability pillars

Their answer — Agentic Harness Engineering — is a closed loop driven by three matched pillars:

PillarWhat it doesWhy it matters
Component observabilityGives every editable component a file-level representationThe action space becomes explicit and revertible
Experience observabilityDistills millions of trajectory tokens into a layered, drill-down corpusThe agent consumes signal, not raw logs
Decision observabilityPairs every edit with a self-declared prediction, verified laterEvery edit becomes a falsifiable contract

Together they turn editing into a testable hypothesis: "I predict this change lifts pass@1; the next round's outcome confirms or rejects it." That is the ablation method (Module 09) automated and scaled.

Section 3The result, and what it means

Empirically: ten AHE iterations lifted pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2 from 69.7% to 77.0%, surpassing the human-designed Codex-CLI harness (71.9%) and self-evolving baselines. The frozen evolved harness transferred: +5.1 to +10.1pp cross-family gains across three other model families, suggesting the evolved components encode general engineering experience, not benchmark tuning. Ablations localize the gain to tools, middleware, and long-term memory — not the system prompt. That last point is the same lesson as Module 09: factual harness structure transfers; prose strategy does not.

The AHE loop: every edit is a prediction, verified against the next round's outcome.

Section 4HarnessX: composable + evolvable

Chen et al. (HarnessX, arXiv:2606.14249) approach from the other side: assemble typed harness primitives via a substitution algebra (composable), adapt them through AEGIS, a trace-driven multi-agent evolution engine (adaptive), and close the loop by turning trajectories into both harness updates and model-training signal (evolvable). Across five benchmarks it reports an average +14.5% gain (up to +44%), largest where baselines are lowest — i.e. evolution helps most when you're worst off.

The two papers agree on the load-bearing insight: agent progress need not come from model scaling alone. Composing and evolving the runtime interface from execution feedback is a complementary, actionable lever.
